    Ordering at a café

    Sample A2, Standard

    You ordered, coped with a sold-out item and paid without switching to English. Three sentences are worth another look.

    Questions that already have a question word

    Grammar
    You said
    Duōshao qián ma?多少钱吗？
    Better
    Duōshao qián?多少钱？How much is it?
    Why
    多少 already makes it a question, so adding 吗 asks it twice.

    是 before adjectives

    Grammar
    You said
    Zhèlǐ shì hěn piàoliang.这里是很漂亮。
    Better
    Zhèlǐ hěn piàoliang.这里很漂亮。It's really nice here.
    Why
    Adjectives act as the verb in Chinese. Link them with 很, not 是.

    Measure words for drinks

    More natural
    You said
    Wǒ yào yí ge nátiě.我要一个拿铁。
    Better
    Wǒ yào yì bēi nátiě.我要一杯拿铁。I'd like a latte.
    Why
    个 is understood, but drinks usually take 杯 (cup). The barista used it in their reply.

    Correct as it was

    Left alone

    Bīng de, yào yànmài nǎi.冰的，要燕麦奶。Iced, with oat milk.

    Short and natural. Nothing to change.

    Pattern to watch

    Adding 吗 to questions that already use 什么, 哪儿 or 多少.

    Built to keep you talking.

    It stays in character.

    Convo is the barista, not a teacher. It won't stop the conversation to correct you, and it handles mistakes the way a patient person would.

    It corrects afterwards.

    Mistakes are saved quietly and explained in your report. Meanwhile, Convo's replies use the correct form, so you hear it in context.

    It leaves correct sentences alone.

    If what you said is right, it isn't marked wrong just to look thorough. Only real mistakes and genuinely more natural phrasings make the report.

    Convo runs on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, a small open model, so its corrections aren't taken on trust. Before launch they'll be measured against a test set of learner sentences with known mistakes, including correct ones it should leave alone.

    Coming next: say it out loud. Voice input and spoken replies are on the way, then more everyday conversations beyond the café.

    Good questions.

    Do I need to know some Chinese already?

    A little. Convo is built for A1–A2 learners: you know greetings, numbers and some everyday words. If you're near the start, choose Easy and keep pinyin and English switched on.

    Will Convo correct me in the middle of a conversation?

    No. It replies the way a real barista would, often using the correct form so you hear it. Corrections wait for your report at the end.

    Is the conversation scripted?

    No. Replies are generated as you go, so the conversation follows what you actually say. The conversation on this page is a fixed sample so you can see how it works.

    Can I choose what I see?

    Yes. Show characters, pinyin, English or any combination, and change it whenever you like. The toggles on this page work the same way.

    Can I speak instead of typing?

    Not yet. Convo starts with text. Voice input and spoken replies are the next things being built.

    Which AI model is behind it?

    Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, an open model. It's small, so its corrections will be measured against real learner mistakes before launch rather than assumed to be right.

    Why only one conversation for now?

    Starting with one everyday situation makes it possible to get it right before adding more. Ordering a drink is first; more scenarios follow.
